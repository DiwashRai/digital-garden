## What are they?
Well here's my own definition:
> [!quote] Definition
>   A repeatable set of actions taken to achieve a goal in an optimised and consistent manner.

## Why?
Because there are things that you regularly do, that might be backed by a lot of experience or
science, but you don't want to have to think about all the time. You just want the intended outcome
and not to waste time thinking about "how was it that I do that thing again"?.

Imagine how annoying and frantic it would be to always think about how you should start your work
day every day.

-   Did I check my emails? Or should I check them later to give people a chance to send them out
    in the morning? Check them once a day? Twice? Thrice!?
-   Oh I have a code review. What should I check first? Just read it randomly and hope I spot
    something? Or should I check for logical errors first. What about style...

People talk about decision fatigue and whilst that may be a thing, for me the benefits of workflows
is a few things:
-   Not wasting time thinking over decisions that you've made countless times before.
-   Being explicit so you don't forget crucial steps.
-   Refining the workflow to improve the outcome gradually over time.
