## Preamble
The linked video seemed to touch on something that I had heard of and sort of subconsciously knew
but I didn't explicitly think about. It seems like an evolution of mental models and something to
keep in mind. Having frameworks for various different tasks/domains seems extremely useful.

## What are they?
People throw around the term framework quite a lot and it's meaning seems flexible. It can be used
in the phrases 'legal framework', 'cultural framework', 'software framework' etc.

What I mean can probably be through more of as an 'analytical framework'. Here is my own definition
for that:

> [!quote] Definition
>   A structured method to analyze, understand and make decisions or take actions around a
>   specific domain

I don't know if this is a good definition but it makes sense to me. When people hear 'framework',
they may think of something more 'grand/official' or established. Something like the SWOT analysis
system. But, to be honest, I'm intersted in making every frameworks for tasks that I regularly do.

For example things that are more common but might not have official frameworks are:
-   Code reviews. Everyone probably has a subconscious set of checklists or things they do but they
    don't think about explicitly as a framework.
-   Buying/renting a house. Another thing that a lot of people do and probably develop their own
    methods for screening possible places.

## Why though?
Simple. So that if you're asked a question about how you approach something, you have an easily
accessible answer to provide instead of just "gut feeling".

Having a system be explicit in your mind also allows you to iterate on it and improve it.

And finally, once it's explicit you don't have to reconstruct a process/method/system from scratch
every time you do a similar task.
